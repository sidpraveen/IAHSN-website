Understand HS

HS is a chronic inflammatory skin condition, and support can change the experience.

Symptoms can look different from person to person, and delays in diagnosis are common. IAHSN focuses on clear education, supportive conversations, and practical next steps.

Symptoms and Signs

HS can show up as painful bumps, draining sores, and recurring flares.

You deserve clear language and compassionate support. HS often appears in areas where skin rubs together, including the underarms, groin, inner thighs, buttocks, and under the breasts.

Tender nodules Recurring abscesses Drainage or odor Sinus tracts Scarring Flare cycles
Emotional Wellness

The emotional weight of HS is real, and support should feel steady.

Pain, uncertainty, stigma, and flares can affect confidence, relationships, school, work, and daily routines. Emotional wellness means making room for care that is compassionate, practical, and never judgmental.

What to Notice Patterns matter
  • Repeated painful lumps in the same body areas
  • Sores that drain, heal slowly, or return after healing
  • Tunnels under the skin or thickened scar tissue

Tracking symptoms can make clinician conversations easier and help people feel less alone.

Support Check-In Care for the whole person
  • Name what feels hard without minimizing it
  • Build a flare plan for rest, school, work, and care
  • Connect with people who understand the HS experience

Emotional support can help transform isolation into steadier, more confident next steps.

What is HS?

A chronic, non-contagious inflammatory condition that may cause painful lumps, abscesses, drainage, and scarring.

Read More

Common Myths

HS is not caused by poor hygiene, is not contagious, and is never something a person should be blamed for.

Read More

What We Know

Research points to immune, genetic, hormonal, bacterial, and lifestyle factors that can influence HS.

Care Options

Treatment is individualized. Dermatology care, wound support, pain planning, and mental health support can all matter.

Diagnosis Delays

HS is often mistaken for boils or infections. Better awareness can help people get answers sooner.

Mental Health Hub

Emotional support for the parts HS can make hard to name

Living with HS can affect confidence, relationships, routines, and emotional health. These topics create a starting point for care and conversation.

Emotional Impact

Chronic pain, flare-ups, drainage, and stigma can lead to stress, frustration, embarrassment, grief, or isolation.

Recognizing those feelings can make it easier to ask for the right kind of support.

Coping Strategies

Journaling, pacing, mindfulness, symptom tracking, and supportive routines may make daily life more manageable.

Small tools can still be meaningful when they are realistic for your life.

Body Image

Scarring and symptoms in sensitive areas can affect confidence, intimacy, clothing choices, and social comfort.

Supportive conversations can help rebuild trust in your body over time.

Community Support

Peer support, advocacy groups, and patient stories can reduce isolation and help people feel seen.

Connection is not a replacement for care, but it can be a powerful part of it.

Seeking Help

A licensed mental health professional can help with anxiety, depression, trauma, stress, relationship strain, or emotional fatigue.

Asking for help is a practical step toward feeling less alone.
