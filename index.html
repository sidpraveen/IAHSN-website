What is HS?

HS is a chronic, non-contagious inflammatory skin disease characterized by painful lumps, abscesses, and scarring

HS Myths

  1. HS is not caused from poor hygiene
  2. HS is not contagious
  3. HS is not your fault!

HS Facts

  1. HS can be managed with care
  2. Early treatment can reduce flare-ups
  3. You are not alone!

What HS is Not

HS does not define who you are. Many people with HS build resilience, find support, and continue to thrive every day.

Why diagnosis is often Delayed

HS can take time to recognize, but growing awareness is helping more people receive answers, care, and support sooner.

It looks different for everyone

HS affects everyone differently, and every experience is valid. You are not alone, and support at IAHSN is always available.

Mental Health Hub

Living with HS can affect more than just the body. This hub is here to support emotional wellness, confidence, and connection.

Emotional Impact

Living with Hidradenitis Suppurativa can take a toll on emotional well-being. Many individuals experience stress, frustration, embarrassment, and isolation due to chronic pain, flare-ups, and the stigma surrounding the condition.

Recognizing these feelings is an important step toward building support, resilience, and self-understanding.

Coping Strategies

Healthy coping tools can make day-to-day life more manageable. Practices like journaling, mindfulness, stress reduction, and creating supportive routines may help reduce emotional strain.

Finding small, realistic strategies that work for your life can make a meaningful difference over time.

Body Image & Confidence

HS can affect confidence and self-image, especially when symptoms or scarring appear in sensitive areas. Many people struggle with feeling comfortable in their own bodies or in social situations.

Building confidence is a process, and no one should have to go through it alone.

Support & Community

Connection can be a powerful part of healing. Support groups, advocacy organizations, and shared patient experiences can remind people living with HS that they are not alone.

Community creates space for encouragement, understanding, and shared strength.

When to Seek Help

If HS begins to significantly affect your mental health, speaking with a licensed mental health professional may help. Therapy, counseling, and other forms of support can provide tools for handling anxiety, depression, stress, and emotional fatigue.

Reaching out for help is a sign of strength, not weakness.
