Emotional Impact

Chronic pain, recurring flares, drainage, and stigma can contribute to stress, frustration, embarrassment, grief, and isolation.

Recognizing these emotional challenges is an important first step toward finding the support that's right for you.

Coping Strategies

Journaling, pacing, mindfulness, symptom tracking, and supportive daily routines can help make life with HS more manageable.

Small, realistic steps can make a meaningful difference over time.

Body Image

Scarring and symptoms in sensitive areas can affect body confidence, intimacy, clothing choices, and social interactions.

Compassionate support and open conversations can help people rebuild confidence and reconnect with their sense of self over time.

Community Support

Peer support, advocacy organizations, and patient stories can help people feel understood, connected, and less alone. While they don't replace medical care, they can be an important part of living well with HS.

Connection is not a replacement for care, but it can be a powerful part of it.

Seeking Help

A licensed mental health professional can provide support for anxiety, depression, trauma, stress, relationship challenges, and the emotional impact of living with HS.

Seeking help is a practical and courageous step toward caring for your overall wellbeing.
