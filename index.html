Understand HS

HS is a chronic inflammatory skin condition. The right support can make living with it less overwhelming.

HS symptoms can vary from person to person, and delays in diagnosis are common. IAHSN provides trusted education, supportive guidance, and practical resources to help you take your next steps with confidence.

Symptoms and Signs

HS can cause painful lumps, draining lesions, and recurring flares that can affect daily life.

HS most commonly develops in areas where skin rubs together, including the underarms, groin, inner thighs, buttocks, and beneath the breasts. You deserve clear, easy-to-understand information and compassionate support every step of the way.

Tender nodules Recurring abscesses Drainage or odor Sinus tracts Scarring Flare cycles
Emotional Wellness

The emotional impact of HS is real. You deserve compassionate support, trusted information, and a community that understands.

Pain, uncertainty, stigma, and recurring flares can affect confidence, relationships, school, work, and everyday life. Emotional wellness includes compassionate, practical support that meets you where you are—without judgment.

What to Notice Patterns matter
  • Repeated painful lumps in the same body areas
  • Sores that drain, heal slowly, or return after healing
  • Tunnels under the skin or thickened scar tissue

Keeping track of your symptoms can help you recognize patterns, prepare for conversations with your healthcare provider, and remind you that you're not alone in this journey.

Support Check-In Care for the whole person
  • Acknowledge what feels difficult without minimizing your experience.
  • Build a flare plan for rest, school, work, and care
  • Connect with people who understand what it's like to live with HS

Compassionate support can help turn isolation into connection and uncertainty into confident next steps.

What is HS?

HS is a chronic, non-contagious inflammatory skin condition that can cause painful lumps, abscesses, drainage, and scarring

Read More

Common Myths

HS is not caused by poor hygiene, is not contagious, and is never something a person should be blamed for.

Read More

What We Know

Research suggests that HS develops through a combination of immune, genetic, hormonal, and environmental factors.

Read More

Care Options

Treatment for HS is individualized because every person's experience is unique. Your care plan may include dermatology care, wound care, pain management, and mental health support.

Read More

Diagnosis Delays

HS is often mistaken for boils, ingrown hairs, or skin infections. Greater awareness can help people recognize the signs earlier and seek appropriate medical evaluation.

Read More

Different Stories

HS affects everyone differently. Every person's experience is valid, and support should meet people where they are.

Mental Health Hub

Emotional support for the challenges of living with HS that are often hard to put into words.

Living with HS can affect confidence, relationships, daily routines, and emotional wellbeing. These resources are designed to help you better understand those challenges and start meaningful conversations with your healthcare provider and support network.

Emotional Impact

Chronic pain, recurring flares, drainage, and stigma can contribute to stress, frustration, embarrassment, grief, and isolation.

Recognizing these emotional challenges is an important first step toward finding the support that's right for you.

Coping Strategies

Journaling, pacing, mindfulness, symptom tracking, and supportive daily routines can help make life with HS more manageable.

Small, realistic steps can make a meaningful difference over time.

Body Image

Scarring and symptoms in sensitive areas can affect body confidence, intimacy, clothing choices, and social interactions.

Compassionate support and open conversations can help people rebuild confidence and reconnect with their sense of self over time.

Community Support

Peer support, advocacy organizations, and patient stories can help people feel understood, connected, and less alone. While they don't replace medical care, they can be an important part of living well with HS.

Connection is not a replacement for care, but it can be a powerful part of it.

Seeking Help

A licensed mental health professional can provide support for anxiety, depression, trauma, stress, relationship challenges, and the emotional impact of living with HS.

Seeking help is a practical and courageous step toward caring for your overall wellbeing.
